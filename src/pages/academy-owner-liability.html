What a martial arts academy owner should have on record

This is not legal advice, and liability rules vary by state and by what actually happened. What follows is about the record an owner has, or doesn't, when something goes wrong and the question becomes "what were you doing about it."

The question that gets asked

When an incident happens at any youth activity, the questions that follow are rarely only about the incident itself. They tend to include what the organization knew, what it had trained its staff to do, and what standards it held itself to before anything happened. An owner's memory of good intentions does not answer these questions; a record does.

What a record looks like

  • Dated proof that each coach was screened before they started teaching, not after.
  • Certificates for abuse prevention, mandated reporter, and CPR and first aid training, each with its date and expiry.
  • A written code of conduct that coaches and staff acknowledged, not just a policy that existed somewhere.
  • A log of any concern raised, what was done about it, and when.

What the absence of a record looks like

No dates. No signed acknowledgment. Training that happened once, informally, with nothing kept on file. An owner who is confident the academy does things right but cannot produce anything that shows it. None of this means an academy is unsafe; it means that if the question is ever asked, there is nothing to hand over except an answer that has to be taken on trust.

What to write down first

Owners who start this work usually stall because they try to write everything at once. There is an order that makes it finishable. Start with the code of conduct, because it is the document every other one refers back to. Then the incident response steps, because that is the one you will need under pressure and least want to be drafting at the time.

After those, record what each coach has completed and when it expires. A credential nobody can locate is functionally the same as one that was never obtained. Last, write down who is responsible for checking each of these, by role rather than by name, so the process survives someone leaving.
