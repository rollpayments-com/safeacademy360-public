What is searched, and by whom

Screening searches the public sex offender registries of the fifty states, the District of Columbia, and the US territories, and public records, through Offendersearch, a search provider that Safe Academy 360 uses for this purpose. Offendersearch is not affiliated with Safe Academy 360 or Roll Payments beyond that.

The search is name based. It returns possible matches with a confidence score, the basis for the match, the registry or source each record came from, a link to the public record, and the time that source was last checked. It also reports which sources answered. An incomplete search is never presented as empty.

Offendersearch states that it is not a consumer reporting agency and that its results are not a consumer report. Safe Academy 360 does not compile reports about people. It passes the academy links to public information for the academy's own review.

At registration

When a person registers with the academy, the search runs automatically as part of the signup they already complete. Nobody at the academy has to chase paperwork or remember to run it. Screening is disclosed in the registration agreement the person accepts before it runs.

The result goes to the academy, not to the person at the front desk in the moment, and not to anyone else.

At coach onboarding

Each coach is screened before anything else in the certification path. Screening is disclosed to the coach and consent is captured before it runs. A coach who does not pass review does not proceed to the trainings.

Nothing about an individual coach's screening is published anywhere: not in the directory, not on verify, not on a badge or certificate, and not by implication. Public surfaces show academy status and credential counts only.

What the academy sees

  • Which sources answered, and which did not. When a source could not be searched, the result says so in plain words, and an absence of matches from the sources that did answer is not treated as an answer from the ones that did not.
  • Any possible matches, each with its score, the basis for the match, the source, the link to the public record, and when that source was last checked.
  • When there are no possible matches: the statement that no matching records were returned by the sources that answered. Not "clear," not "passed," not "safe."

An absence of matches is not a guarantee. Registry data is incomplete, matching is name based, and a person who is not in a registry is simply absent from it. That sentence appears beside every result the academy sees, in the same size as the result.

What the person agrees to

The registration agreement discloses that the registration will be checked against public sex offender registries and public records, names the purpose, and captures consent before the search runs. The same is true of coach onboarding. Consent is never pre ticked.

How to ask what was found, and how long results are kept, is set out in the privacy policy.

What the data cannot tell you

It cannot tell you that a person has no record. Registries list people who were required to register and were entered; many offenses never lead to registration, and records lag. Public records coverage varies by jurisdiction.

It cannot tell you that a match is the same person. Common names produce false matches. A match is a candidate for a person to review against the linked record, not a finding.

It cannot answer for a source that did not respond. When a registry could not be searched, the result says so, and nothing about that registry can be inferred.

It cannot make anyone safe. Screening reduces risk. No screening catches everyone, and a certified academy is still an academy where adults and children share a mat, which is why the questions any parent should ask still apply.

On a possible match

Three things are fixed. A person looks at the linked public record before anything happens. Safe Academy 360 makes no automatic decision, and the academy makes any decision only after that individual review. And no outcome is published.

The written process for a possible match, including who at the academy is notified, what the academy is expected to do, and how the person can correct a mistaken match, will be published on this page before the first academy is certified.
