Background check or registry screen? The difference, honestly

Owners and parents use "background check" for anything that looks up a person. The phrase covers two different things, and an academy should know which one it is running and say so.

What a background check is

In the sense employers mean it, a background check is a consumer report. A consumer reporting agency compiles it about a named person, usually from a Social Security number, date of birth, and address history, and searches county and federal court records, often with a person pulling files by hand. Federal law governs it. The person has to be told in writing and has to authorize it, and if the report leads to a decision against them, a set process with notices and a waiting period applies.

That is the right tool for a hiring decision about an employee. It takes days, and sometimes weeks. It costs money per report. And it produces a document about a person that the academy then holds and is responsible for.

What a public records screen is

A public records screen searches public sources for a match on a legal name and date of birth. At Safe Academy 360 the sources are the public sex offender registries of all 58 US jurisdictions and state prison system records, searched through a provider that holds a continuously updated copy of them. The answer comes back in seconds, which is what lets it run at the moment a coach is added or a registration lands rather than as a separate errand.

The result is not a report about the person. It is either no matching records from the sources that answered, or one or more possible matches, each with the basis for the match, the source, and a link to the public listing. A possible match is a candidate for a person to look at. What happens when a screen returns a match sets out that review in full.

It does not search county court or federal records. Those are not part of the screen, and an academy describing it should say so.

Before a coach is screened, the academy confirms in writing that it has told the coach and the coach has agreed. Registration screening is disclosed in the registration agreement. No result about any individual is ever published.

What each one misses

Neither tool catches everyone, and the honest comparison is about which gaps each one has.

  • A background check reflects the day it was run. Most academies never repeat it, so a conviction the following year is invisible until someone orders a new report.
  • A background check searches the jurisdictions the agency was asked to search. A record in a county nobody named is not in the report.
  • A registry screen is name based. A different spelling or a name change can miss a real record, and a common name can flag the wrong person.
  • A registry screen only sees what was registered or recorded through a formal process. Conduct that was never reported is in neither tool.
  • Some sources do not answer a given search. Safe Academy 360 shows the academy which ones did not and runs the screen again, rather than treating silence as an empty result.

Why Safe Academy 360 runs a screen and repeats it

The program runs the public records screen because it can run on everyone, immediately, and again. Every coach is screened when they are added. The adult who holds a registration account is screened the moment the registration lands. Everyone screened is screened again each month, so a registry entry added after signup surfaces within weeks rather than at the next hiring cycle, if ever. Why the monthly repeat matters.

None of that stops an academy from ordering a consumer report on a coach it is hiring as an employee. Many should. The two tools answer different questions, and an academy that runs both has a stronger record than one that runs either.

How to describe either one

Do not say a person passed, was cleared, or was vetted. Say what was searched and what came back: no matching records were returned by the sources that answered. Say when it ran and whether it will run again.

Do not call a registry screen a background check. Parents hear the phrase and picture the employer version. An academy that names what it actually searches is easier to trust than one that rounds up.
