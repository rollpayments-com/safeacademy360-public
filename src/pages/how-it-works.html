How Safe Academy 360 works

The 360 is coverage around the whole operation, not a checklist an owner completes once. Two directions are stated here in full, including what each one does not do. It applies to any martial arts academy: jiu jitsu, judo, wrestling, MMA, karate, taekwondo.

Certification of the academy and its coaches

What it covers

The people who teach, and the standards they teach under. Every coach at a certified academy has been screened against public records, has completed SafeSport, mandated reporter, and CPR and first aid training, and has each certificate on file. The academy has adopted a written code of conduct and is listed publicly so a parent can check.

For a parent, the point is peace of mind grounded in something specific. For an owner, the point is documented good faith leadership: if the question "what were you doing about it" ever comes, the answer already exists in writing.

What the academy does

  • Names every coach who teaches, including assistants and anyone who leads a kids program.
  • Adopts the code of conduct and keeps it where students and parents can read it.
  • Makes sure each coach completes the trainings with the providers and uploads each certificate.
  • Renews each credential before it expires. The platform shows the dates ahead of time.

What Safe Academy 360 does

  • Screens each coach against public records before anything else. A coach who does not pass review does not proceed.
  • Receives each uploaded certificate, reviews the document, and records it against the coach with its own expiry date.
  • Tracks every credential to its own expiry, and shows a coach as lapsed when any one of them expires.
  • Issues the academy's certificate number, lists the academy in the directory, and answers a certificate lookup on verify.
  • Acts as an independent third party. The academy does not certify itself, and Safe Academy 360 is not owned by any academy, affiliation, or sanctioning body.

What it does not do

It does not verify a certificate with the body that issued it. An uploaded certificate is a document that was reviewed and kept on file. Read it that way.

It does not guarantee anyone's conduct. Screening and training reduce risk. They do not make a person safe, and no screening catches everyone.

It does not license anyone, and it is not a government program. Certification is a private program's statement that its requirements were met as of a date.

It does not publish anything about an individual coach's screening. Public surfaces show academy status and credential counts only.

Screening at registration

What it covers

Who walks in the door. When someone registers with the academy, the registration is checked against public records and sex offender registries as part of the signup they already complete. Almost no academy addresses this today, and it is the exposure a coach credential cannot reach: adult students share the mat and the changing room with children.

What the academy does

  • Uses the registration flow that carries the screening. Screening is disclosed in the registration agreement the applicant accepts.
  • Reviews any possible match a person at the academy is responsible for reviewing. A match is a candidate for review, not a verdict.
  • Decides what to do. Safe Academy 360 does not make that decision for the academy.

What Safe Academy 360 does

  • Runs the search automatically at registration, so nobody chases paperwork.
  • Shows the academy which sources answered and which did not.
  • Shows possible matches with a score, the basis for the match, and a link to the public record, so a person can look at the source before doing anything.

What it does not do

It does not prove anyone has no record. An absence of matches means the registries that answered returned no matching records for the name searched. Registry data is incomplete, matching is name based, and when a source does not answer, the search is silent on it. That is stated to the academy each time, not summarized away.

It does not compile a report about a person. It surfaces links to public information for the academy's own review.

It does not decide who may join. That is the academy's decision, made by a person, after looking at the record.

It does not catch everyone. Common names produce false matches, and a person who is not in a registry is simply absent from it.

The full description of screening, including what the applicant agrees to and what happens on a possible match, is on the screening page.

The record

Both directions leave a record: who was screened and when, which trainings were completed and when they expire, which standards the academy adopted, and which registrations were checked. The record is what turns "we take safety seriously" into something an owner can show.
